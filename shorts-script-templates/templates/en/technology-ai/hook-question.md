# Template: Hook + Question + Reveal + CTA

**Estimated duration:** 25–40 seconds  
**Format:** Voiceover / talking head  
**Retention:** High — open question creates a cognitive loop the brain wants to close

---

## Structure

```
[HOOK — question that sounds impossible or ridiculous]
[ANTICIPATION — "most people would say X, but..."]
[ANSWER — the truth in 2–3 sentences]
[IMPLICATION — what this means for the viewer]
[CTA]
```

---

## Script

**[HOOK]**  
[Question about technology or AI that sounds counterintuitive or sparks curiosity]?

**[ANTICIPATION]**  
Most people would say [obvious/wrong answer]. But the real answer is [unexpected — 1 sentence].

**[ANSWER]**  
[Explanation of the mechanism in max 2 sentences, no technical jargon]. This happens because [simple cause].

**[IMPLICATION]**  
What that means for you: [practical consequence the viewer can act on or that directly affects them].

**[CTA]**  
[Question inviting them to share their own experience with the topic].

---

## Filled example

**[HOOK]**  
Can an AI know it's lying?

**[ANTICIPATION]**  
Most people would say no — AIs just repeat what they were trained on. But the answer is more uncomfortable than that.

**[ANSWER]**  
Language models can generate a wrong answer while internally producing signals that indicate uncertainty. They "know" in some sense — but say it anyway. Not because they want to deceive you, but because they're trained to give an answer, not to stay silent.

**[IMPLICATION]**  
What that means for you: when an AI responds confidently, that's not a signal it's correct. Confidence in tone is a training artifact, not a sign of certainty.

**[CTA]**  
Has an AI ever given you something totally wrong but completely confident? Tell me below.

---

## Usage notes

- The hook question works best when it challenges something the viewer assumes about technology
- The anticipation ("most people would say X") creates identification before the reveal
- Avoid terms like "LLM", "tokens", "parameters" — if it can't be said another way, it's not for Shorts
- This format works especially well for AI content because the audience has many assumptions to break
