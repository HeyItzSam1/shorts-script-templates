# Template: Hook + Mini Story + Twist + CTA

**Estimated duration:** 35–55 seconds  
**Format:** Voiceover  
**Retention:** Very high — story + twist keeps viewers watching until the end

---

## Structure

```
[HOOK — reveal the twist upfront]
[SETUP — minimum context]
[TENSION — the critical moment]
[TWIST — the unexpected outcome]
[CONNECTION TO TODAY]
[CTA]
```

---

## Script

**[HOOK]**  
In [year/era], [person or group] [action that sounds incredible or impossible]. And it changed everything.

**[SETUP]**  
[Name/group] was [one-sentence description]. At the time, [context explaining why the situation was extreme or unlikely].

**[TENSION]**  
When [the critical moment arrived], [what was at stake]. The decision was [action they took].

**[TWIST]**  
What nobody expected: [surprising outcome in 1–2 sentences].

**[CONNECTION]**  
Today [real consequence that is still alive: a law, an invention, a city, a tradition].

**[CTA]**  
Did you know this story? Drop it below.

---

## Filled example

**[HOOK]**  
In 1907, a French chef accidentally invented one of the most used flavor enhancers in history while trying to recreate a taste from seaweed soup.

**[SETUP]**  
Kikunae Ikeda was a chemistry professor in Tokyo obsessed with understanding why his wife's dashi broth tasted so uniquely satisfying. He called that taste "umami."

**[TENSION]**  
After months of experiments, he isolated the compound responsible: glutamate. The question was whether it could be produced at scale and sold as a seasoning.

**[TWIST]**  
Within a year, he had patented it and launched a product: monosodium glutamate — MSG. It spread across Asia and then the world in under a decade.

**[CONNECTION]**  
Today MSG is in thousands of packaged foods worldwide, still following the same basic formula Ikeda developed in that Tokyo lab.

**[CTA]**  
Did you know MSG was invented by a chemistry professor chasing a taste? Drop your reaction.

---

## Usage notes

- Reveal the twist in the hook — it creates a loop that pulls viewers through
- Keep setup to one sentence — only what's needed to understand the tension
- Max 2 proper names per script to avoid cognitive overload
- The connection to the present is what makes this more than a trivia fact
